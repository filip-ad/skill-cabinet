## Design Context

### Users
A single operator at a desk: someone who has accumulated hundreds of agent skills across Cursor, Claude, Codex, and other tool chains. They open this cabinet to inventory what is installed, read a skill before trusting it, and delete cards that should not be there.

### Brand Personality
Quiet, precise, librarian. The interface should feel like a working catalog, not a product. Confidence comes from order and legibility, not decoration.

### Aesthetic Direction
Archival card catalog: warm paper, iron-gall ink, brass drawer hardware, a faded ledger rule. Light (paper) only. Type is the furniture. Anti-references: SaaS dashboards, neon-on-dark “dev tools,” Inter, identical icon-and-caption card grids, glassmorphism.

### Design Principles
1. The skill body is the object — give it a readable measure and keep chrome out of the way.
2. Scopes are drawers; skills are cards; metadata is cataloguing, not a settings panel.
3. Destructive action is a delete stamp, not a cute confirm.
4. Density is allowed in the drawers; emptiness belongs to the reading surface.
5. Every label should sound like a library, not a startup.
